The AI That Babysits Qubits

A quantum computer is a choir where every singer slowly drifts off key — and looking directly at any singer makes them forget the song. Error correction is a conductor who fixes pitch without ever hearing anyone sing solo: they listen to pairs of neighbors and infer who drifted.

For 30 years, humans wrote the conductor's rulebook by hand. In 2024, Google DeepMind replaced the rulebook with an AI (AlphaQubit) that learned a real choir's specific bad habits from its data. It makes about 30% fewer wrong calls than the hand-written rulebooks fast enough to run on a live machine, and about 6% fewer than the slowest, most careful one — though it's still too slow to conduct live itself; making it fast is the current race.

The thing AI is worst at (being exactly right) is being fixed by the thing quantum is worst at existing without (error correction). That loop is why you're here.
