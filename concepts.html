🟡 Intermediate — real terms, light math

The AI That Babysits Qubits

Physical qubits fail constantly (~1 error per 1,000 operations on good hardware). The fix: encode one logical qubit across many physical ones — a surface code, a grid where data qubits are interleaved with measurement qubits.

The measurement qubits run parity checks called syndrome measurements: they tell you something flipped, and roughly where, without touching the data. A decoder then infers the most likely error pattern from the syndrome history — like a doctor diagnosing from symptoms.

The classic algorithm (minimum-weight perfect matching) assumes errors are independent and simple. Real hardware isn't: errors correlate, leak, and drift. AlphaQubit is a recurrent transformer trained first on simulations, then fine-tuned on the real chip's data — so it learns the actual noise, not the idealized model. Result: ~30% fewer logical errors than the best real-time classical decoders on Google's hardware.
