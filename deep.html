The AI That Babysits Qubits

Rotated surface code with code distance d: d² data qubits, d²−1 syndrome qubits; stabilizers are weight-4 X/Z plaquette operators in the bulk (weight-2 on boundaries), measured each cycle. Decoding = inference over the syndrome volume: given syndrome history σ, find the most probable logical equivalence class of errors.

MWPM solves this on a decoder graph with edge weights from an assumed independent-Pauli + phenomenological noise model — provably good under that model, degraded under correlated noise, leakage, cross-talk, and non-Markovian drift.

AlphaQubit: recurrent transformer over per-round syndrome inputs (including analog "soft" readout information rather than binarized measurements — a key advantage), pretrained on hundreds of millions of simulated samples, fine-tuned on thousands of experimental samples from a Sycamore processor. Reported ~6% fewer logical errors than tensor-network decoders (near-optimal but too slow for real time) and ~30% fewer than correlated matching, maintained up to d=11 (241 qubits) in simulation.

pL ≈ A (p / pth)⌊(d+1)/2⌋

Below threshold, a decoder that effectively raises pth or sharpens the exponent compounds through every future distance increase — decoder quality is hardware you don't have to build.

The real-time frontier: "NN decoders are too slow for the ~1 μs cycle" is no longer simply true — an FPGA neural decoder achieved 550 ns system latency (124 ns network inference) inside a 1.25 μs QEC cycle on superconducting hardware (May 2026). Scaling learned decoders to large d in real time remains open.
