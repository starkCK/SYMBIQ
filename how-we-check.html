How we check

What checks a number before it reaches this site, and what no check can catch.

The short version

AI drafts most of this site: the prose, the code, the first version of every figure. Scripts then recompute whatever can be recomputed. Fresh AI sessions try to break each page. One person, Chinmoy, decides what goes live. None of that is a guarantee. The corrections log holds 20 entries, and each one is a case where all three layers missed something.

Where a number comes from

A figure that carries weight comes from the primary source: the paper, the standard, the regulation or the press release itself. A summary by someone else is a lead, not a source. If we cannot reach the primary source, we do not publish the figure, and the gap goes in a log the desk keeps.

Layer 1: scripts

Scripts do the parts that do not need judgement.

  • Arithmetic is redone. Widget outputs, thresholds, resource estimates and game pars are recomputed, usually by trying every possible input rather than a hopeful handful. A game par that is not a proven optimum is labelled “best known”, not “par”.
  • Counts are read, not typed. How many topics a course has, how many corrections are logged, how many claims the Ledger tracks: the number on a page is written into it from the thing it describes.
  • Charts and tables come from data files. The Bitcoin timing chart and the hardware table are generated from the papers’ figures and the hardware data, so a page cannot drift from its data.
  • Citations are looked up. Every arXiv identifier is checked against arXiv: does the paper exist, was it withdrawn, has a newer version changed the figure we quote.
  • The site guards itself on every publish. Links, file hashes and the content-security policy are checked before anything goes out.

What scripts cannot catch. A sentence that is wrong but contradicts no table. A source that says something other than what we say it says. A correct number for the wrong quantity. Most of the corrections below were that kind of error.

Layer 2: AI referee passes

A fresh AI session, with no memory of writing the page, is told to attack it: recompute every number, fetch every cited source, and look for claims that go further than the evidence. These passes have found real errors, including several in the corrections log.

What they cannot catch. They can share blind spots with the AI that drafted the page. They can be confidently wrong. They are only as good as the source they manage to fetch.

Layer 3: a person

Chinmoy decides what is published and acts on reader reports. He is one person with limited hours, and he does not re-derive every proof himself. That is why layers 1 and 2 exist, and why this page does not call the result “verified” without saying by what.

Receipts

The figures a reader is most likely to act on carry a receipt: a small note that opens beside the number and shows the formula, the inputs, the source, the date it was last checked, and what would prove it wrong. Look for the underlined figures on pages such as the Bitcoin page.

Evidence tiers

Every claim carries one of four tiers, so a reader can see what kind of knowledge it is:

  • Proven — a theorem, or a measured result with a citation.
  • Heuristic — works in practice, no guarantee.
  • Inspired — a faithful analogy, not the real mechanism.
  • Frontier — an open question; nobody knows yet.

How the Ledger judges a claim

The Ledger tracks20 dated, public, falsifiable claims. The resolution criteria and the sources that will settle each one are fixed the day it is logged. A verdict is written after the deadline and checked against those criteria. The verdict words have no term for lying or hype: they set what was said next to what happened. If a claim turns out to be unscoreable, that counts against us.

When we are wrong

It goes on the corrections log, in public, with more room than the original mistake had: what we said, what is true, and how we missed it. The log counts anything wrong that reached the published site, whoever found it. A reader who finds one can have their name on it.

Known gaps

Only3 of the20 topics on The Machinery link a primary paper so far, so most of that course is checked by scripts and AI passes but not cited. Some older pages still carry numbers typed by hand, and each one we find gets moved to a computed figure and logged as a correction.
