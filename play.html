The Arcade

Five of these six machines are the ones you meet on the Solver's Path — here, no story, no mentor, no order. The sixth, The Calibration, has no mission of its own; it lives here and natively on circuits.html. Just the games, and the physics doing the judging.

Push the door

Behind it: six machines that will not let you fake a score. Where a machine has a par — Circuit Golf, Grover's Escape, Max-Cut — that par is a proven optimum found by exhaustive search, not a designer's guess. The Volcano scores you on a schedule replayed 500 times rather than on a par, because a single lucky run proves nothing. The Calibration scores you against the exact same drift a fixed strategy would have faced, replayed on demand, so luck in the qubit's own drift never gets credited to you. Every probability on every screen is computed rather than chosen. And one landscape is built to prove that your best strategy is worthless on the wrong problem.

The whole campaign, rebuilt as scenes

The same verified engines as the machines below, inside the thing we are building instead of a widget in a box: a world that responds to the number the game is actually computing, and mentors whose flaws are the mechanic rather than a paragraph about them.

Six acts, five to eight minutes each. Sound is off until you ask for it. On The Solver’s Path they run in order and remember what you did; here you can walk into any one of them.

Out in the wild

Six more playable things live on the pages they belong to, because each one only makes sense standing next to its explanation.

The Bench

Three real methods race on the same graph, 200 replays each, at a time budget you set — and the winner changes depending on which end of the slider you're on.

The Decoder Duel

Ten rounds against the textbook matching decoder, on a chip carrying a defect nobody told it about. You sit in the AI's seat.

The CHSH game

Beat a limit classical physics cannot beat — 85.4% against a provable ceiling of 75%.

The Question

One question at a time, answered at three levels, with a streak that forgives a single miss the way a real code corrects a single error.

The Bottleneck

Five upgrades, three resources, and a shadow price that lies to you if you read it without its range. Operations research, on The Feasible Region.

The Prune

Prove which haul is best by opening about thirty-five branches out of thousands — and finish holding a certificate rather than a guess.
